\begin{defbox}[Stationarity]
A time series $(X_t)_{t \in \Z}$ is \emph{strictly stationary} if for any
$h \in \Z$ and $t_1, \dots, t_n$, the joint distribution of
$(X_{t_1}, \dots, X_{t_n})$ equals the joint distribution of
$(X_{t_1+h}, \dots, X_{t_n+h})$.
\end{defbox}

\begin{thbox}[Wold decomposition]
Any covariance-stationary process can be written uniquely as the sum
of a deterministic component and an infinite moving-average process
of uncorrelated innovations.
\end{thbox}

\begin{exbox}[White noise]
$X_t \iid \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$ has $\E[X_t] = 0$, $\Var[X_t] = \sigma^2$,
and $\rho(h) = 0$ for all $h \neq 0$.
\end{exbox}

\begin{rembox}
Stationarity is the foundation of classical time-series modelling.
ARCH/GARCH (Chapter~6) handle the case where conditional variance is
itself time-varying.
\end{rembox}
