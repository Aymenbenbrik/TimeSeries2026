\begin{exercise}[TS vs.\ DS by simulation]\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
Both series exhibit an upward drift around $0.05 t$, but their
deviations behave very differently:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
                          & TS: $Y_t = 0.05t + \varepsilon_t$ & DS: $Z_t = Z_{t-1} + 0.05 + \eta_t$ \\
\midrule
OLS-detrended residual sd & $0.882$ & $3.112$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The TS detrended residual is essentially $\mathcal N(0,1)$ noise; the
DS residual still wanders because OLS detrending cannot remove the
unit root --- it only removes a deterministic line.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[ADF test]\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Series                             & ADF stat & $p$-value & Decision at $5\%$ \\
\midrule
$Y_t$ (TS, regression \texttt{ct}) & $-9.098$ & $0.0000$  & \textbf{reject} $H_0$ -- stationary \\
$Z_t$ (RW+drift)                   & $-0.687$ & $0.8500$  & fail to reject -- unit root \\
$\Delta Z_t$                       & $-13.507$ & $0.0000$ & \textbf{reject} -- stationary \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The ADF test correctly identifies $Y_t$ as stationary around a trend,
$Z_t$ as having a unit root, and one differencing as enough to remove
the unit root.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[KPSS test]\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Series                             & KPSS stat & $p$-value & Decision at $5\%$ \\
\midrule
$Y_t$ (TS, \texttt{ct})            & $0.092$  & $\geq 0.10$ & fail to reject -- stationary \\
$Z_t$ (RW+drift, \texttt{c})       & $1.789$  & $\leq 0.01$ & \textbf{reject} -- unit root \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

ADF and KPSS \emph{agree} on both series:
\begin{itemize}
  \item For $Y_t$: ADF rejects unit root \emph{and} KPSS does not
        reject stationarity $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{trend-stationary}
        (top-left cell of the combination matrix).
  \item For $Z_t$: ADF fails to reject unit root \emph{and} KPSS
        rejects stationarity $\Rightarrow$ \textbf{difference-stationary}
        (bottom-right cell).
\end{itemize}
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[Differencing the random walk]\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
The empirical ACF tells the story:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
              & ACF at lags $1$--$5$ \\
\midrule
$Z_t$         & $0.982,\ 0.963,\ 0.946,\ 0.929,\ 0.913$ \\
$\Delta Z_t$  & $+0.039,\ -0.069,\ -0.008,\ -0.000,\ +0.024$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$\rho_Z(h)$ decays \emph{linearly} (the unit-root signature), while
$\rho_{\Delta Z}(h)$ is essentially zero --- one differencing has
turned the random walk into white noise.

Re-applying the tests on $\Delta Z_t$:
\[
  \text{ADF: stat}=-13.51,\ p=0.000\quad\text{(reject unit root)},
  \qquad
  \text{KPSS: stat}=0.101,\ p\geq 0.10\quad\text{(stationary)}.
\]
Both tests now agree on stationarity. The series $Z_t$ is
$I(1)$ and $\Delta Z_t \sim WN$.
\end{solution}

\begin{exercise}[SARIMA on Souvenir sales]\end{exercise}

\begin{solution}
\textbf{(1)--(2)} On the raw $\log Y_t$ ($T = 84$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
Test                & Statistic & $p$-value & Decision \\
\midrule
ADF, regression \texttt{ct}    & $-1.046$ & $0.938$ & fail to reject -- unit root \\
KPSS, regression \texttt{ct}   & $+0.117$ & $\geq 0.10$ & fail to reject -- stationary \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The two tests are \emph{ambiguous} but consistent with the
near-unit-root behaviour expected from a strongly trending,
seasonal economic series. We proceed with one regular and one
seasonal differencing.

\textbf{(3)} SARIMA$(0,1,1)(0,1,1)_{12}$ fitted by maximum
likelihood (\texttt{statsmodels}):
\[
  \widehat\theta_1 = -0.4991,
  \quad
  \widehat\Theta_1 = -0.3245,
  \quad
  \widehat\sigma^2 = 0.0247,
  \quad
  \text{AIC} = -43.08,
  \quad
  \text{BIC} = -36.95.
\]

\textbf{(4)} Ljung--Box on the residuals at lag $24$:
\[
  Q_{\text{LB}}(24) = 16.04,
  \qquad
  p\text{-value} = 0.887.
\]
We \textbf{do not reject} the white-noise hypothesis: the residuals
have no remaining autocorrelation, so the SARIMA captures the
structure of the series.

\textbf{(5)} The 12-month forecast (figure
\texttt{lab4\_ex5\_forecast.png}) extrapolates the upward trend and
the December peak; the $95\%$ confidence interval widens with the
forecast horizon, as expected for an integrated process.
\end{solution}
